# Spike bead

A spike reduces uncertainty with evidence within an agreed budget. It need not exist for every design choice; a small investigation can remain in the current bead.

1. Read the question, variants and measurements. Compare plausible alternatives under the same relevant conditions; do not invent variants just to fill a template.
2. Use a disposable branch or isolated worktree when experimentation would disturb deliverable work. Stay within the bead/project time and resource budget; absent a budget, start with a short bounded experiment and reassess before expanding it.
3. Record measurements, limitations and the recommendation in the notes. Preserve reproducible commands or harnesses where future work needs them.
4. If the answer is within delegated authority, record it and continue. Otherwise use the `question` skill, with the evidence and alternatives. The spike can close once its own Acceptance Criteria are met and its findings are durable; the decision or implementation remains separate unfinished work.
5. Experimental code is not production-ready by default. Reuse tested pieces when appropriate, subject to normal review and delivery gates; no automatic rewrite-from-scratch requirement. Clean up only your own disposable artifacts when they are no longer needed and cleanup is authorized.

The number of spikes follows uncertainty and the agreed budget, not a one-per-epic quota.
